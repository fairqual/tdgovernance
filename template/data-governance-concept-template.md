***How to use this template***

*The underlying idea of the Data Governance Concept is to make data
governance a functional element of a Td approach that is introduced at
the start of a project for both researchers and practitioners.*

*In a Td process, the generation and the use of the Data Governance
Concept develops through three phases. The three phases shift from
**data governance**, to **data management**, where data are generated
and handled day-to-day, and eventually to **data stewardship** in the
later phases of a project. While these three aspects can be considered
sequentially, they should be discussed and agreed upon in the early
phases of the project when the Data Governance Concept is generated.*

# Data governance plan

*Following questions should guide the creation of a data governance
concept at the early stage of the project. We would suggest addressing
the different items in the following sequence, although the project
design may require another sequence.*

***Phase 1 (raising the discussion):***

- *Who should be part of data governance discussions and decisions in
  your research project?*

- *Who should manage and access research data, between researchers
  and/or practitioners?*

- *Definition of the expected outcomes: what does the project intend to
  produce? What is the purpose and envisioned outcomes of the project
  for practitioners and researchers?*

- *What kind of data will be produced as part of the expected outcomes?*

- *Who could benefit from this data (e.g. practitioners, local
  community, actor groups, future research projects, the research field
  in general)? What specific skills are needed to use these data? How
  will these skills be put into practice, and how far will trainings be
  necessary?*

- *Who coordinates the ethical procedures? This is traditionally a task
  carried out by researchers, but depending on the context of the
  project, this could also be reconsidered.*

- *What mechanisms do we have in place to periodically review and update
  the Data Governance Concept as project scope or requirements evolve?*

- *Who is responsible for ensuring the continuous update and development
  of the Data Governance Concept?*

***Phase 2 (Putting the plan into place):***

- *How will data collection and processing – especially from
  co-production moments – be documented?*

- *How can you ensure that informed consent gives the participants
  agency, without overburdening them?*

- *What degree of anonymization is needed regarding promises made in the
  informed consent, the sensitivity of the data, and the planned
  long-term data stewardship solution?*

- *Who will carry out the anonymization of the data? What tools will be
  used? (If Artificial Intelligence will be used, specify how this will
  be put into place).*

- *What specific complexities or privacy risks make anonymization
  difficult in your current Td process?*

- *Who is responsible to ensure the continuous update and development of
  the data governance concept?*

- *When and how often should researchers and practitioners check, and
  re-align if necessary, the way data is managed?*

***Phase 3 (Re-assessing and next steps):***

- *Where will the data be stored in the long-term?*

- *For what may data be used in the future (e.g. secondary analysis,
  teaching, quality control)?*

- *Who gets to see or reuse the data (during the project / after the
  project)? And who will decide on who can see and reuse the data
  (access management)?*

- *What are sensitive topics for the local context / risks in relation
  to the project? Both in terms of how they will (not) be addressed
  during the project and with regard to potential data sharing?*

- *What could be outlets and/or repositories for the data (if it can be
  shared based on ethical considerations and the participants'
  agreement)? Examples for repositories beyond the scope of academia are
  e.g. community archives, archive of specific organizations, museums,
  public websites.*

# Potential revisions of the Data Governance Concept throughout the Project

*Following questions guide the possible revisions of the Data Governance
Concept at a later stage of the project. These elements may be
provisional at an early stage of the project, but are to be reasonably
expected during the execution of the project:*

- *Will there be changes in the interest in the data from the practice
  side?*

- *Which data will be documented/archived/shared, which not?*

- *Where will the data be archived/shared? (repositories yes/no; local
  archive or database; ...)*

- *What choices are expected to be made by research participants? (e.g.,
  do many want to share or not share their data? What are the
  consequences if only part of the data is available (e.g. in terms of
  de-identification or biased voices)*

- *What access restrictions are needed? Who gets access and who grants
  access?*

- *Legal questions: Are there obligations or requirements from funders
  or practitioners?*

- *How can it be ensured that the data is visible and accessible (if
  meeting defined access criteria) for all people involved?*

- *What mechanisms do we have in place to periodically review and update
  the Data Governance Concept as project scope or requirements evolve?*
